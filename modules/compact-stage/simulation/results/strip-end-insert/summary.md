# strip-end-insert — results

Fit check and membrane model of the strip end concept of Niels Bosmans. Regenerated by:

```bash
python modules/compact-stage/simulation/cases/strip-end-insert/run.py
```

## The short answer

- **The base can carry it.** The groove, the tapped hole and the magnet pockets are all made from
  the top and stay in solid material. The groove keeps 0.75 mm of wall to the half-round pocket in
  the corner. The magnet pocket keeps 1.7 mm to the damper pocket.
- **Sunk flush with the strip, the insert clears everything.** Carriage-top, carriage-bottom, the
  scraper and the top rollers pass over it at the end of the travel and even in a crash. The
  scraper lips pass 0.15 mm above it, as above the strip. No change to the carriage is needed.
- **As drawn, 1.05 mm above the strip, it is hit.** At the end of the usable travel the scraper
  lips and carriage-top touch it. That needs the lips stepped back, as drawn, and the scraper
  pocket in carriage-top made longer at the insert: about 12 mm instead of 10 mm for normal travel,
  about 19 mm for a crash.
- **Under the carriage the pull is even across the width.** When the carriage is in the middle
  of the travel, the pull under it is within 2 % of the average.
- **Near the strip ends the pull runs along the edges.** At the first bottom roller, with the
  carriage at the end of the travel, the strip middle carries 71 % of the average pull and the
  edges 156 %. Within 30 mm of the strip end the middle carries almost nothing. There the strip
  is held flat only by the base and the magnets.
- **Beside the inserts the strip is squeezed sideways,** up to 6.7 MPa. That is about the
  stress at which a free 50 mm wide panel of 0.15 mm steel buckles, so the strip may wrinkle
  there if nothing holds it down.
