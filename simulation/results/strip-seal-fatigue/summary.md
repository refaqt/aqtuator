# strip-seal-fatigue — results

Life of the sealing strip against the bending radius it really takes, the
material and the layout. Regenerated by:

```bash
python3 simulation/cases/strip-seal-fatigue/strip_seal_fatigue.py
```

All values are for a 0.1 mm strip of EN 1.4310 at Rm 2050 unless another figure
is named. One cycle is one crossing of the carriage over one point of the strip.

## The short answer

The rollers in the design are 14 mm and that is fine. The strip does not wrap
them. It turns about 12 degrees at each roller, over a ramp roughly 38 mm long
and 8.38 mm high, and at that angle a 0.1 mm strip bridges a 14 mm roller
instead of following it. The bending stress is then 57 to 362 MPa, against a
fatigue limit of about 1019 MPa.

An earlier version of this case reported 1312 MPa and 19,000 crossings for the
same rollers. Two things were wrong with it, and both are corrected here.

## What changed, and by how much

| | Old | Now | Why |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Fatigue limit at 0.1 mm | 775 MPa | 1019 MPa | 775 MPa is the value at 0.25 mm. Thinner strip is stronger |
| Stress on a 14 mm roller | 1312 MPa | 57–362 MPa | The strip bridges the roller, it does not wrap it |
| Smallest wrapped roller, both ways | 23.8 mm | 18.1 mm | Follows from the corrected fatigue limit |
| Crossings on 14 mm rollers | 19,000 | no limit reached | Follows from both corrections together |

## The fatigue limit against thickness

Alleima publish ±775 MPa at 0.25 mm and ±630 MPa at 0.50 mm, both at Rm 2100,
bending transverse to the rolling direction, 50 percent survival at 2 million
cycles. That is a power law with exponent 0.299.

| Thickness | Fatigue limit | Source |
| ---: | ---: | --- |
| 0.50 mm | 630 MPa | measured |
| 0.25 mm | 775 MPa | measured |
| 0.15 mm | 903 MPa | extrapolated |
| **0.10 mm** | **1019 MPa** | **extrapolated** |
| 0.08 mm | 1090 MPa | extrapolated |
| 0.05 mm | 1254 MPa | extrapolated |

Everything under 0.25 mm extends the maker's own trend past the thinnest strip
they measured. It has to be confirmed with Alleima before the design leans on
it. Three questions for them are listed at the end of this page.

## Does the strip wrap the rollers?

A strip takes the roller radius only if something presses it there over an arc.
A thin strip under light pull spreads the turn over a length `sqrt(EI/T)` on
each side of the roller and bridges it. For a 0.1 mm strip the bending
stiffness is 15.4 N·mm² per mm of width, which is stiff compared with the pull
a cover strip carries.

Rollers 14 mm, turn 12.4 degrees at each one. The pull needed before the strip
takes the roller radius is **26.3 N per mm of width, which is 1,053 N on a
40 mm strip**. Nothing in a cover strip comes close to that.

| Pull in the strip | Radius | Stress | Wraps? | Share of the limit |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | :---: | ---: |
| 0.05 N/mm | 161.8 mm | 57 MPa | no | 0.06 |
| 0.10 N/mm | 114.4 mm | 81 MPa | no | 0.08 |
| 0.20 N/mm | 80.9 mm | 114 MPa | no | 0.11 |
| 0.50 N/mm | 51.2 mm | 181 MPa | no | 0.18 |
| 1.00 N/mm | 36.2 mm | 256 MPa | no | 0.25 |
| 2.00 N/mm | 25.6 mm | 362 MPa | no | 0.35 |
| 5.00 N/mm | 16.2 mm | 572 MPa | no | 0.56 |

The design variable is therefore the **turn angle**, which comes from the ramp
length and the lift height. The roller diameter is only a floor on the radius.

## What one carriage pass costs

| Pull | Peak stress | Cycles per pass | Crossings |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.2 N/mm | 114 MPa | one full reversal | no limit reached |
| 2.0 N/mm | 362 MPa | one full reversal | no limit reached |

**One pass is one full reversal.** Not four, and not one per roller. The strip
returning to zero stress between two rollers does not close a cycle. This part
of the earlier work was right and has not changed.

## Worst case: if the strip did wrap every roller

Keep this table for the case where a roller is pressed onto the strip, or where
a future layout turns the strip much more sharply.

| Roller | Stress | Share of proof strength | Crossings |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 14 mm | 1312 MPa | 0.66 | 79,000 |
| 16 mm | 1149 MPa | 0.58 | 430,000 |
| **18 mm** | **1022 MPa** | **0.52** | **1,900,000** |
| 20 mm | 920 MPa | 0.47 | no limit reached |
| 24 mm | 768 MPa | 0.39 | no limit reached |

Permanent set is never the limit at this temper. Even 14 mm reaches only two
thirds of the proof strength.

A thinner strip is the other way out of the same corner. The allowable stress
itself depends on thickness, so these are solved by iteration rather than in
one step.

| Roller | Thickness needed | Order |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 14 mm | 0.082 mm | 0.08 mm |
| 18 mm | 0.100 mm | 0.08 mm |
| 20 mm | 0.108 mm | 0.10 mm |
| 24 mm | 0.124 mm | 0.10 mm |

Thinner strip helps twice over: less thickness in the stress, and a higher
fatigue limit. It also dents more easily, and denting is what ends a cover
strip in the field. The design stays at 0.10 mm.

If the strip ever does wrap, opening only the two rollers above the carriage
still helps, because it shrinks the downward peak and turns the reversal back
into a one way bend: 14 mm with 40 mm gives 8 times the life, and 14 mm with
60 mm gives 13 times. A longer ramp does the same job without the height, by
lowering the turn angle.

## Materials, on a fully wrapped roller

| Material | Standard | 0.1 mm in stock | Bent one way | Bent both ways |
| --- | --- | :---: | ---: | ---: |
| Alleima 11R51 | EN 1.4310 / AISI 301 | yes | 13.5 mm | **18.1 mm** |
| Alleima Hiflex | UNS S42026 | no, from 0.152 mm | 16.8 mm | 22.7 mm |
| IPCO 1850SM | AISI 1065 carbon | no | 17.9 mm | 24.3 mm |
| Alleima Springflex | EN 1.4462 duplex | no | 21.4 mm | 32.4 mm |
| IPCO 1200SA belt | EN 1.4310, soft temper | no | 26.8 mm | 38.6 mm |

Only 11R51 has a published thickness trend, so only its figures are corrected
to 0.1 mm. The others are quoted at the thickness their maker measured, which
flatters 11R51's rivals. EN 1.4310 still wins, and for the same reason as
before: its modulus is the lowest of the group at 185 GPa, and bending stress
is proportional to modulus.

## What other makers do

No maker of linear axes publishes a deflection roller diameter, and the one
whose design is documented in detail uses no rollers at all. HIWIN lift the
cover strip over the carriage with plastic sliding guides, eight per carriage,
and they publish the length this adds to the carriage at each end:

| Axis | Deflection length per end |
| --- | ---: |
| HT100 | 30 mm |
| HM040, HM060 | 40 mm |
| HM080 | 45 mm |
| HT150, HT200 | 47.5 mm |
| HM120 | 60 mm |
| HT250 | 70 mm |

Those lengths are the same order as the 38 mm ramp in this design, which is the
real point of comparison. An SMC patent makes the opposite case explicitly: in
its description of earlier designs it says a seal band guided by guide rollers
"is flattened when it is guided by the rollers".

## How the seal actually reaches the end of its life

Not by cracking. HIWIN's own instruction is to change the cover strip "as soon
as there are any signs of rippling and it can no longer be held in position by
the magnetic strips", and they say the strip wears by rubbing. Parker reject a
band for "nicks, kinks, and damage to the sharp edge". So the strip is a wear
part that is inspected, and the fatigue calculation only proves that fatigue is
not what ends it.

## Why travel in kilometres does not measure this

A point of the strip is loaded once each time the carriage crosses it. One back
and forth over a stroke `s` is `2s` of travel and gives two crossings:

```
travel [m] = crossings to failure x stroke [m]
```

The same strip lasts twenty times further on a long stroke than on a short one,
so a bare kilometre figure describes the duty cycle, not the strip. For scale,
Parker rate the stainless band on their OSP-P rodless drive at 8,000 km.

State the life as **crossings of the carriage over any one point of the strip**,
together with the inspection rule above. The controller already knows every move
it commands, so it can count crossings per position without extra hardware.

## Still open

- **The real radius at a roller.** Everything on this page rests on the strip
  bridging the 14 mm rollers. Measure it on the built carriage with a radius
  gauge or a photograph against a scale.
- **The ramp length.** 38 mm is read off a CAD sketch and has to be confirmed
  against the model.
- **The pull in the strip.** The table above walks a range because this number
  is not known. Measure it.
- **Three questions for Alleima.** Is there measured fatigue data at 0.1 mm?
  What is the value along the rolling direction, which is how this strip bends?
  What is the value at a lower survival rate than 50 percent?
- **Coolant.** Every fatigue value here is measured in dry air. The stage runs
  SAMNOS AL, a water based coolant at pH 7.4, applied as minimum quantity
  lubrication. A bend test with the real strip and the real coolant is still
  needed, and the chloride content of the coolant is still not on its data sheet.
