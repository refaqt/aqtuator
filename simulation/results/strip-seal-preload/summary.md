# strip-seal-preload — results

Bending radius and pull of a 0.25 mm seal strip over four 10 mm rollers, for the layout in the
carriage sketch. Regenerated by:

```bash
python3 simulation/cases/strip-seal-preload/strip_seal_preload.py
```

## The short answer

- **304 does not work at 30 mm.** At 30 mm the 0.25 mm strip carries about 750 MPa. The
  strongest 304 strip on sale starts to take a permanent bend at about 760 MPa, and its
  fatigue limit is estimated at 420 to 620 MPa. A 0.25 mm strip of 304 needs a radius of
  36 to 54 mm.
- **301 at 30 mm is right at its limit.** The stress is 771 MPa against a measured fatigue
  limit of 775 MPa, and that limit is for 50 percent survival. There is no margin.
- **Pull does not make the 30 mm arcs.** More pull always makes the bend tighter. The strip
  takes 30 mm only when the pull is at most 0.36 N per mm of width, which is 15 N on a 40 mm
  strip.
- **Fixed ends would pull far too hard.** The path over the carriage is 2.4 mm longer than
  a flat strip. If both ends are screwed down, the carriage stretches the strip and adds 56
  to 223 N per mm of pull, depending on the strip length. One end needs a soft spring.
- **Smaller than 30 mm costs life.** With 301 at 0.25 mm, 25 mm gives about 420,000
  crossings and 20 mm about 60,000. The strip takes a permanent bend below 11.7 mm.

## The radius each material needs, 0.25 mm strip

| Material | Stress at 30 mm | Smallest radius, no fatigue limit | Smallest radius, no permanent bend |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1.4310 / 301, 11R51 at Rm 2050 | 771 MPa | 30 mm (measured data) | 11.7 mm |
| 1.4301 / 304, class +C1300 | 746 MPa | 36 to 54 mm (estimate) | 29.4 mm |

The 304 limit for a permanent bend uses the minimum yield of half hard 304 in ASTM A666.
EN 10151 does not give a yield value for its classes.

## What the pull does, layout as drawn

| Pull | On a 40 mm strip | Tightest radius | Highest point of the strip | Extra path length |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.1 N/mm | 4 N | 32.7 mm | 13.8 mm | 3.03 mm |
| 0.2 N/mm | 8 N | 31.5 mm | 12.7 mm | 2.72 mm |
| 0.5 N/mm | 20 N | 29.0 mm | 10.8 mm | 2.25 mm |
| 1.0 N/mm | 40 N | 26.6 mm | 9.4 mm | 1.96 mm |
| 2.0 N/mm | 80 N | 23.6 mm | 8.2 mm | 1.76 mm |
| 5.0 N/mm | 200 N | 19.2 mm | 7.1 mm | 1.61 mm |
| 10.0 N/mm | 400 N | 15.7 mm | 6.7 mm | 1.55 mm |
| 20.0 N/mm | 800 N | 12.3 mm | 6.4 mm | 1.50 mm |

The tightest radius is always where rollers 1 and 4 press the strip onto the profile. The
strip is flat on one side of that point and must turn on the other, so the point acts like a
clamp. Under low pull the strip arches above the rollers between roller 2 and roller 3. It
has 15.75 mm of room there.

## Layout changes, at 0.36 N/mm

| Change | Tightest radius | Strip leaves the profile |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| As drawn | 30.0 mm | at roller 1 |
| Rollers 1 and 4 lifted 0.5 mm off the strip | 60.1 mm | 11.8 mm before roller 1 |
| Rollers 1 and 4 lifted 1.0 mm off the strip | 86.3 mm | 21.5 mm before roller 1 |
| Ramp 1.5 times longer, 39 mm | 61.4 mm | at roller 1 |
| Rise 4 mm instead of 6 mm | 44.6 mm | at roller 1 |

The carriage end sits about 10 mm before roller 1. With lifted rollers the strip leaves the
profile just outside the carriage, so the seal opens there. The magnets are not in the model
and will shorten that length. A longer ramp or a lower rise opens the bend without that
problem.

## Going below 30 mm with 301, 0.25 mm

| Radius | Stress | Crossings before a crack |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 35 mm | 661 MPa | no limit |
| 30 mm | 771 MPa | no limit, no margin |
| 27.5 mm | 841 MPa | 980,000 |
| 25 mm | 925 MPa | 420,000 |
| 22.5 mm | 1028 MPa | 170,000 |
| 20 mm | 1156 MPa | 60,000 |
| 15 mm | 1542 MPa | 4,800 |

One crossing is one pass of the carriage over one point of the strip. The finite life values
follow a straight line on a log scale between 0.9 Rm at 1,000 cycles and the fatigue limit at
2 million. They show the trend, not a guarantee.

A thinner strip is the other way down. Radius scales with thickness, and thin strip has a
higher fatigue limit. At 0.10 mm, 301 reaches no fatigue limit down to about 9 mm.
