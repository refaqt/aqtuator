How this is calculated

The strip is a thin beam under a pull. The page finds the shape with the lowest energy: bending energy plus the work of the pull, with the exact curvature. Rollers 1 and 4 hold the strip down, rollers 2 and 3 hold it up, and the magnet strips are an elastic bed that pushes, plus a small magnetic pull near the strip. Nothing forces the strip onto the rollers, so the radius comes out of the solution. This is the solver from simulation/cases/strip-seal-preload/, ported to JavaScript and checked against it.

Screw preload. The strip is fitted with loose screws and lies over the rollers with no pull. A screw travel u must then be taken up by a flatter arch and by stretching the steel: u = extra(0) − extra(T) + L·T / (E·t). The arch is a soft spring, so at low pull most of the travel goes into the arch. That is why the arch height is a good gauge for the pull. The arch height is measured from a straight edge on the tops of rollers 2 and 3 to the underside of the strip, at its highest point. It is zero when the strip runs flat over the two rollers.

Fatigue. One crossing of the carriage over a point of the strip gives that point the whole stress profile above once. The history is cut into cycles by rainflow counting, the pull adds to the mean, Goodman corrects for the mean, and a Basquin line through 0.9 Rm at 10³ and the fatigue limit at 2·10⁶ gives the life (50 % survival). Below the fatigue limit the life has no limit.

Assumptions.

  • The pull is the same along the whole strip: it slides on the magnets while the screw is tightened.
  • The carriage is far from both clamps. Near a clamp the pull can change.
  • The rollers turn freely. No friction.
  • Magnet strip stiffness and pull are estimates. The stiffness sets the force on rollers 1 and 4 and changes the tightest radius, so measure it or try the range 5 to 1000 N/mm³.
