End-stop crash of the compact stage: results and proof

What happens when the carriage runs into the end of its travel at the speed limit of 2 m/s, and the motor keeps pushing with 670 N? This page shows, for one case at a time, the models that were used, the loads, the 3D stress in the base, the time history of the crash, and every check that shows whether the numbers can be trusted.

The models, and how they were built

Two models work together. A crash model of the moving carriage gives the forces over time. A finite element model (FEM: the part is cut into many small pieces and the solver finds the stress in each) of the base gives the stress and the lasting dent. The FEM also gives the stiffness of the end wall back to the crash model. The other parts are checked with hand formulas from the makers' catalogues.

1. Crash model: one rigid body on four springs

All moving parts (carriage halves, motor coil unit, guide blocks, read head, payload) are one stiff body. It can move and turn in all 6 directions. Each part has its own mass at its own place from the CAD model.

m·a = F_motor + F_bumpers + F_wall + F_blocks + F_gravity + F_magnet J·α = sum of the moments of the same forces about the centre of mass

Guide blocks: 4 springs, 115 N/µm up/down and sideways (HIWIN QE15, preload ZA). Free along the travel.

Bumper, per bumper (ACE TUBUS TA17-7, stroke s = 7 mm), with compression d:

F(d) = F_full · (1 − (1 − d/s)^1.8) for d ≤ 7 mm F_full = 6 J / (s · (1 − 1/2.8)) = 1333 N (6 J at full stroke) F rises with 3.6 kN/mm past 7 mm (flat tube; not known, swept) moving back: only 35 % of the force (65 % of the work → heat)

Metal hit: 2 mm after the full bumper stroke, the end face of the carriage hits the end wall. The wall force follows the force–deformation curve of the FEM (with yield). It springs back with its elastic slope, so the plastic part stays as a dent.

Motor: 670 N towards the wall for the whole crash (worst case), or 0 N (motor switched off). Start: the carriage touches the bumpers at the full speed (2.0 or 2.2 m/s).

Solver: time steps of 2 µs or smaller (4th order Runge–Kutta), until the carriage comes to rest or bounces away.

2. FEM of the base end

Supports: the two bottom strips that sit on the machine table (y ≤ 40 mm and y ≥ 170 mm) are held fixed in all directions. The middle of the bottom has a 0.5 mm recess and does not touch the table.

Two separate load cases: (a) pocket: the bumper force as a pressure on a 20 mm disc on the back of each pocket; (b) band: the metal hit as a pressure on the inner face of the end wall, z = 24 to 40 mm.

Material: EN AW-7075-T651, minimum values to EN 485-2. Elastic up to Rp0.2 = 460 MPa, then a straight line to Rm = 530 MPa at 4.5 % plastic strain. The elongation at break is 5 %.

Solver: CalculiX 2.23, large deformations on, the load raised in 16 steps.

Why a static model is enough: the end wall vibrates at Hz (period ms). The bumper force builds up over several ms, which is much slower, so static is fine. The metal hit lasts about 0.5 ms; there the static answer is only an estimate.

3. Hand checks of the other parts

Guide block: P = |F_up/down| + |F_side| + C0·(M_roll/M0x + M_pitch/M0y + M_yaw/M0z) safety = C0 / P C0 = 8790 N, M0x = 70 N·m, M0y = M0z = 30 N·m (HIWIN QEH15SA). HIWIN asks safety ≥ 3 to 5 under shock. Read head: peak acceleration at the head position, |a + α × r|, against 1000 m/s² (RSF, 6 ms half sine). Screw joint: clamp force F_V = M_A / (0.16·P + 0.58·d2·µ_G + 0.5·D_Km·µ_K) (VDI 2230, µ = 0.12) slip use = side force / (µ_joint · sum of the clamp forces after the pull) above 1 the joint slips µ_joint = 0.10 (0.15 also shown), 10 % clamp force lost by settling, class 8.8 table torques.

How to read the 3D picture

  • Only the start end of the base (x = 0 to 100 mm) is in the model. The far end carries no crash load. The flat face at x = 100 mm is the cut.
  • Purple: the faces that carry the load. Grey stripes at the bottom: the fixed supports.
  • The deformation is drawn larger than it is (slider), so you can see the shape. The numbers are not scaled.
  • The stress the solver gives at a corner node is extrapolated. Where the metal yields, it can show more than the material can carry (above 530 MPa). Then read the plastic strain map: it shows the real yielding.
  • Mesh: 2nd order tetrahedra, 1.2 mm at the pocket walls, 3 mm in the end wall, 6 mm elsewhere.

Time plots of the crash

Time 0 is the first touch of the bumpers. Dashed horizontal lines are limits.

Two things you will see, and why. (1) At the deepest point the bumper force drops at once to 35 %. That is how the model handles the bumper turning work into heat: on the way back it only gives back 35 % of the force. A real elastomer drops fast, but not in zero time. It does not change the peak values. (2) After the stop, the guide block loads keep swinging. The model has no damping in the guide blocks, so the swinging does not die out. Real blocks damp it. This is on the safe side.

Points I found while checking this study

Next steps that would make the result firmer

  1. Ask ACE for the force curve of the TA17-7 past its 7 mm stroke, static and dynamic. It moves the results more than anything else.
  2. Ask ACE what the 9 J emergency rating means for the force curve (see the points above).
  3. Ask RSF whether a 1 ms shock of 1500 to 2500 m/s² is allowed on the AK MC 15 read head.
  4. Weigh the first carriage, and set the screw torques in the drawings.
  5. A drop or crash test on the first stage, with an accelerometer on the carriage, at a low speed first. It would check the bumper force and the peak deceleration of the model directly.
