How the page works

The machine. The lower stage moves along the spindle axis (Z). The upper stage sits on its carriage and moves the tool into the part (X). The tool holder sits on the upper carriage. Both stages are compact stages: MAXWELL MK21 linear motor, four HIWIN QEH15SA blocks with medium preload (ZA) on EGR15U rails.

Stiffness at the tool tip. Two parts add up in series.

  • The position loop of each motor, the same model as the cutting force page: one rigid mass, a digital controller at the loop rate, tuned with the same robustness rules (sensitivity peak at most 6 dB, phase margin at least 30°, gain margin at least 6 dB). The upper motor moves the upper carriage and the tool holder. The lower motor moves everything on the lower blocks.
  • A model of the two carriages as rigid bodies on their guide blocks, and the tool shank. Each block is a spring up and down and a spring sideways. Along its travel each carriage is held by its motor, which is already in the first part. The tilt of each carriage moves the tool tip too, because the tip sits high above the blocks. A downward cutting force therefore also pushes the tip along X.

Forces. Kienzle: Fc = kc1 · b · h1 − mc. The chip width b is the length of the cutting edge in the cut, on the nose and on the straight edge. The chip thickness is h = ap · f / b. The thrust Ft = (Ft/Fc) · Fc acts at right angles to the line between the two ends of the cutting edge in the cut.

Chatter. Each turn cuts the wave the last turn left. The loop is 1 + b · Kt · (1 − e−jωT) · Φ = 0, with T one spindle turn, Kt = dFc/dh per mm of chip width, and Φ the tip compliance seen from the direction of the chip thickness, under the force direction. The lobes come from the same function as the lobes of the cutting force page. The limit chip width is turned back into a depth of cut through the edge shape.

Sources. kc1 and mc: ISCAR material table, hardened steel 60 HRC (4700 N/mm², 0.25). A row for 62 HRC is not given. Block stiffness: HIWIN catalogue GW-13-1-EN-2606-K, Table 3.44 (QE15S, ZA: 115 N/µm). The sideways stiffness is not in the catalogue. Masses and block positions: the compact stage CAD, as in the end stop crash case. Ft/Fc: an estimate. Published values for chamfered CBN inserts lie around 1.1 to 1.5 with a sharp tool, and rise with wear (not confirmed from the paper itself).

Limits.

  • The motor loop and the guide blocks are added in series. The loop does not see the guide modes, and the guide modes do not see the loop. Near 450 Hz both act together, so the real answer may differ.
  • The carriage masses sit in one point each; their tilt inertia comes from boxes of about their size.
  • No process damping. At low cutting speed the flank of the insert rubs on the wave and damps it. In hard turning this helps, so the page is on the safe side.
  • Kienzle is used far below its 1 mm chip thickness. At 30 µm the real force can differ a lot. Measure it.
  • The clamp of the shank in the holder, the holder on the carriage and the screws are rigid here.
  • The damping ratios are estimates. They decide the height of every peak.
