Mechanical power, force times stage speed. Negative mean: the controller takes energy out.

Power cannot be made unitless per newton: it grows with the square of the force amplitude. Twice the force gives four times the power. So the power chart uses the force amplitude set on the left. The mean force does no work in the steady state, because the stage does not move on average. This is the mechanical power at the stage only. The motor heat comes from the current, which follows the force (mean force included), and it is not in this chart.

How this is calculated

Plant. The stage is a single rigid mass: G(s) = 1/(m·s²), from force in N to position in m. The controller force is held constant over each sample (a zero-order hold). The discrete plant is G(z) = T²(z + 1) / (2m(z − 1)²), with T = 1/fs. This already holds the lag of the hold, about half a sample.

Controller. C(s) = K · 1/s · (s + 2πfi)(s + 2πfd) / (s + 2πflp)², with fi = fb/10, fd = fb/3 and flp = 4·fb. Each factor is translated to discrete time with the bilinear rule (Tustin), s = (2/T)(z − 1)/(z + 1). K is set so that |C·G| = 1 at fb, and fb is searched so that the phase margin is 30°.

Why fb follows the loop rate and not the mass. With these frequency ratios, the continuous loop has about 38° of phase margin at any bandwidth. The sampling lag takes the other 8°, and that lag grows with fb/fs. So fb ends at about fs/23, or about fs/69 with the extra computation delay. A heavier stage only needs a larger K. The stiffness near fb is then about m·(2πfb)², so the mass and the loop rate both help.

Cutting force. Fd(t) = Fmean + Famp · sin(2π ftooth t), with ftooth = teeth · rev/min / 60. It acts on the mass, next to the controller force. So Y/Fd = G/(1 + G·C) is the compliance, and its inverse is the stiffness.

Vibration. The amplitude comes from a time simulation, not from the transfer function. The mass moves in continuous time, and it is integrated exactly between samples. The controller runs once per sample. The amplitude is half of the peak-to-peak motion after the start transient, including the motion between samples. Below about fs/4 this agrees with compliance × Famp within a few per cent. Above half the loop rate, the discrete transfer functions have no meaning, but the simulation still holds.

Controller force and power. The controller force u is the output of the controller after the hold. The force ratio is the amplitude of u over Famp in the steady state. Below half the loop rate it follows U/Fd = −C·G/(1 + C·G): about 1 well below fb, where the controller takes the whole force, a peak near fb, and falling above it, where the mass takes the force. The power is u·v, with v the stage speed. Its mean is exact, because u is constant over each sample, so each sample adds u·Δx of work. A positive mean is real. At low speed the integrator pushes energy in, and with the computation delay the lag does the same far above fb. Near fb the controller takes energy out, like a damper.

Limits.

  • The stage is one rigid mass. No resonances, no friction, no guide stiffness.
  • The force is one pure sine plus a mean. A real cut has harmonics of the tooth frequency.
  • No sensor noise, no quantisation, no force limit on the actuator.
  • The cut does not react to the motion, so this model shows forced vibration, not chatter.
